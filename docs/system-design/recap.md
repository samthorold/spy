# Recap

How the learner goes back over what they have noted.

## Recap is recall without rules

A **Recap** shows the learner one side of an **Expression** and lets them recall the other before revealing it. It runs in either direction: Spanish first (recall the Meaning) or Meaning first (recall the Spanish). There is no scoring and no scheduling. The learner chooses what to go over, for example the last week's Entries, and the order is newest first or shuffled.

Recap carries no clever rules because the learner already has them elsewhere. Lingvist does spaced repetition, and class homework sets the pace of the course. A scheduler here would duplicate them and turn the notebook into a third course competing for the learner's time. Recap's job is narrower: bring the learner's own Entries, met in their own conversations, back in front of them.

Both directions are offered because they practise different things. Spanish first is what the learner needs to understand someone; Meaning first is what they need to say it themselves, which is harder and is what conversation demands.

## Only Expressions with a Meaning are recapped

An Expression without a Meaning has nothing to recall, and a **Tip** has no Meaning by nature, so neither appears as a recall prompt.

## Unenriched Entries are surfaced, not skipped

Recap puts the Entries still waiting for **Enrichment** in front of the learner instead of passing over them silently. An Expression captured without its Meaning is knowledge the learner has only half captured. If nothing brings it back, it stays half captured and is effectively lost, and the moment that would have given it its Meaning (asking the teacher, asking the speaker again) passes. Recap is the point where the learner is already going back over their notebook, so it is where the gap is shown.
